# IRC SP 41 (1994): Verified Extract for the Pattom Project

**Date:** 14 October 2026 · **Status:** key pages read and checked against the page images; the rest of the book was not read in detail.
**Register entry:** the registered source for saturation flow, capacity, PCU factors, fixed-time cycle and green-split formulas and warrants (D11, D14, D15; PRD section 8.1). A value this book does not give is a gap.
**Source:** Indian Roads Congress, Special Publication 41, "Guidelines for the Design of At-Grade Intersections in Rural & Urban Areas", 1994. The file was supplied by the guide (user statement, 14 Oct 2026). The copy is a scan; page numbers below are the printed book pages. Whether a later revision exists has not been checked.

## 1. What the book is
A design guide for at-grade intersections. Most chapters are geometry: curves, kerb radii, islands, channelisation, sight triangles, drainage. Those chapters are outside the project scope (ledger D2, timing only). The parts that apply are below.

Contents: 1 Introduction · 2 Basic design principles · 3 Design data · 4 Parameters of intersection design · 5 Capacity of intersection (p. 60) · 6 Traffic control devices (p. 62) · 7 Signal controlled intersection (p. 70) · 8 Special considerations in urban areas (p. 81) · 9 Lighting, drainage, utilities · Appendix I curves (p. 88) · Appendix II capacity assessment, UK practice (p. 105) · Appendix III capacity of unsignalised intersection (p. 113).

## 4. Other values
- Signalised intersection justified, besides other warrants, if the major street has 650 to 800 vehicles per hour (both directions) and the minor street 200 to 250 vehicles per hour in one direction (p. 3).
- Grade-separated intersection justified, besides other warrants, above 10,000 PCU/h across all arms (IRC 92-1985, quoted p. 3).
- Approach-road design capacity: 2-lane one-way 1,400 to 2,400 PCU/h (700 to 1,200 per lane); 2-lane two-way 900 to 1,500 PCU/h (450 to 750 per lane) (p. 61). These are road capacities, not signal saturation flows.
- Quick signal check (Appendix II section 4, p. 107): add the maximum flow per lane of each of the two phases and compare with a practical capacity of 1,600 PCU/h per traffic lane (examples 800/800, 1,000/600, 1,200/400). Described for semi-rural cases with simple counts.
- Peak hour factor is defined as the peak-hour count divided by four times the highest 15-minute count (p. 61). Capacity depends on width, one- or two-way operation, parking, turning movements, truck and bus share, local factors at signals, peak hour factor, pedestrians and cyclists (p. 61).
- India has very little measured capacity data for mixed traffic; the book suggests estimating flows theoretically and adjusting with actual information (p. 61).
- Minimum spacing between intersections: arterial 500 m, sub-arterial 300 m, collector 150 m; signals on arterials should preferably be linked (p. 81).

## 5. Cautions in the printed text
- Section 7.6.3 (p. 78): "The minimum cycle length recommended is preferably 120 seconds being the maximum acceptable delay for drivers of vehicles and pedestrians." The sentence contradicts itself (minimum against maximum). Check IRC 93 before quoting.
- Section 7.6.1.3(iii) (p. 77): the right-turn formula is printed as (g × s) − (g × c) over (s − g); a flow term appears to be missing. Do not use without checking IRC 93 or another source.
- Section 5.3.1 (p. 61) says the HCM-based method is in Appendix III; the contents page calls Appendix III capacity of unsignalised intersections. It applies to priority-controlled junctions, not signals.

## 6. Not in SP 41
- Nothing on emissions or fuel.
- No delay or queue method for signalised intersections (level of service is given only for unsignalised ones).
- No guidance on actuated or adaptive timing; cycle and green design are deferred to IRC 93-1985.
